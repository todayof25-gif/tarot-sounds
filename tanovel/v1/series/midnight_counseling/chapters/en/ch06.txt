It was a night where rain poured as if a giant hole had been punched in the sky.

*Boom- Crash!*

Every time the thunder slammed against the windowpane, the candles illuminating the counseling shop flickered precariously.

"You won't be able to go home today."

Ji-hyuk muttered, looking out the window.
The roads looked like they were about to be submerged.
It was a torrential downpour where visibility was zero even with the wipers moving at max speed.
Grabbing the steering wheel in this weather was tantamount to suicide.

"Sleep here."

Eun-jo said it so casually.
Like a woman who invites a man to stay overnight as easily as asking him to stay for dinner.

"Excuse me?"

When Ji-hyuk asked back in confusion, Eun-jo smirked and pointed to the old leather sofa she was sitting on.

"That sofa is quite spacious. It'll be enough for a big guy like you to lie down alone. Of course, your feet will stick out a bit."

"Ah... you meant that. I misunderstood for a moment."

Ji-hyuk scratched the back of his neck, feeling awkward for no reason.
What was he imagining? His brain must have short-circuited from fatigue.

"What misunderstanding? Did you think I meant sleep together?"

Eun-jo asked mischievously.
Ji-hyuk's ears turned red.

"Would you like another cup of tea? Rosemary is good on rainy days. It improves memory."

Eun-jo changed the subject and put down a teacup.
The pungent and refreshing scent of rosemary mixed with the damp sound of rain to create a strange atmosphere.
The two sat facing each other with the steaming teacup between them.

*Crack, crack.*

Only the sound of the candle burning its wick and the rhythmic sound of rain filled the quiet space.
The silence wasn't awkward.
Rather, this space, which should have been unfamiliar, felt more comfortable to Ji-hyuk than his own home.
Ji-hyuk suddenly became curious about the woman in front of him.

"Eun-jo... why do you run a shop like this?"

"Pardon?"

"You don't take money, you just listen to the complaints of people who can't sleep. You even give out free tea. You're not a philanthropist."

"Hmm... let's just say I'm paying back a debt."

"A debt?"

"I was saved too. By someone."

Eun-jo's gaze drifted to a distant place.
The flickering candlelight reflected in her deep eyes.
She looked so lonely and faint that Ji-hyuk couldn't ask 'by whom' anymore.

"What about you, Detective? Why did you become a detective? Isn't it hard chasing criminals when you can't even sleep?"

This time Eun-jo asked.
Ji-hyuk fiddled with the rim of his teacup.

"There's someone I want to find. I have something I must say when I find them."

"Who?"

"A child I lost 10 years ago."

Ji-hyuk's voice was buried in the sound of rain, small but heavy.

"It was raining like this that day too. There was a fire... and I ran to the rooftop. I met a girl there. A child in a school uniform crying in fear."

The memory came back vividly.
The scorching heat that seemed to burn his skin, the acrid black smoke blocking his vision, and the small, cold hand clutching his clothes.

"I promised to save her. I told her not to worry because the detective would protect her. But... I couldn't keep it. I lost her hand due to the shock of a gas explosion. I still see her falling into the darkness... in my dreams every night, even 10 years later."

Ji-hyuk lowered his head deep.
It was a story he hadn't told anyone, not even his fellow detectives.
The source of his insomnia. The guilt gnawing at his life.
Why was it coming out so easily in front of this strange woman?

"She must be alive."

Eun-jo's voice cut through the darkness firmly.
Ji-hyuk raised his head as if surprised.

"What?"

"That child, she must definitely be alive. You promised. That you'd save her."

"But... it was four stories high. She fell from there... and we couldn't even find a body in the rubble."

"Not finding a body means she walked out alive. Right?"

Eun-jo reached out across the table and placed her hand over Ji-hyuk's trembling fist.
Her hand was cold as ice, but that coldness cooled down Ji-hyuk's heated heart.

"So don't feel guilty. That child won't resent you, Detective. She's probably grateful. You were the only person who held her hand until the last moment. She probably remembers that warmth."

Ji-hyuk looked at Eun-jo blankly.
Her eyes were directed at him without wavering.
Like someone who knew the truth of that day. No, she was filled with conviction like someone who had been at the scene.
Strangely, he believed her words. No, he desperately wanted to believe them.

"Thank you. For saying that."

Ji-hyuk spoke in a wet voice.
Eun-jo gave a silent, profound smile.

*Boom- Crash!*

The building shook entirely at the sudden thunder.
The old ceiling bulb crackled, and then all the lights inside the counseling shop went out completely.
Blackout.

"Uh..."

Pitch-black darkness swallowed the two in an instant.
The only light was a single small candle burning faintly on the table.
In the darkness, the sound of each other's breathing was heard more clearly.
The rain hit the eardrums louder, and the scent of rosemary pricked the nose stronger.

"Are you scared?"

Eun-jo's low voice was heard in the dark.

"Detectives don't catch ghosts, so... well... a little."

Ji-hyuk retorted with a joke, but his back actually felt cold.
The darkness instinctively reminded him of the fear of the smoke-filled stairwell 10 years ago.

"Come here. When the lights go out, you can see other things better."

Eun-jo lifted the candle close to her face.
Under the swaying light, only her face floated up white.
Her black eyes sparkled, holding the candlelight.
It was dreamy, and dangerously beautiful.

"Shall I tell you a scary story? A story about a witch who loved fire so much she became fire herself, a long time ago."

Eun-jo's eyes shone playfully, but somewhat chillingly.
Ji-hyuk swallowed dryly without realizing it.
The sound of rain outside the window grew stronger.
This night, and this strange cohabitation, seemed like it would go on for longer than expected.

(End of Episode 6)
