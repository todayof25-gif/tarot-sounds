The night streets after the rain had stopped were heavy with the smell of wet asphalt.

Ji-hyuk was occupying a corner seat in 'Midnight Counseling' again today.
It was under the pretext of surveillance using his detective title, but in reality, he couldn't forget the taste of that tea.
And he was also curious to find out what this strange woman was planning by running such a shop.

"No guests today."

Ji-hyuk said, fiddling with his empty teacup.
Eun-jo was silently arranging dried flowers behind the counter.
Only the sound of rustling dry petals filled the quiet room.

"Since the rain stopped, someone will come soon. Among those hiding from the rain, there are more people who have nowhere to go because their hearts are wet... than those who are just wet because they don't have an umbrella."

As soon as her words ended like a prophecy, the door opened.

*Jingle—.*

Footsteps so cautious they seemed to be trying to hide their presence.
The guest who entered was a woman in her late 20s.

She was dressed in a neat suit, but her rain-soaked stockings and mud-splattered shoes showed that she had been wandering the streets for quite some time.
She couldn't approach the counter and hesitated at the entrance.

"Welcome."

Eun-jo spoke softly, but the woman didn't answer.
No, she *couldn't* answer.

Her lips moved, but instead of a voice, only rough, wheezing breaths escaped.
She clutched her throat, wearing a pained expression.
Like someone with something sharp stuck in their throat.

"Can you... not speak?"

When Ji-hyuk tried to intervene, Eun-jo stopped him with a glance.
Eun-jo came out from behind the counter and approached the woman.

"It's okay. You don't have to force yourself to speak. Here, silence is also a language."

Only then did tears fill the woman's eyes.
It was the look of someone meeting a person who understood their silence for the first time.
Eun-jo guided the woman to a table.
Then she went into the kitchen and started preparing something with clinking sounds.

A moment later, what Eun-jo brought was a transparent glass cup with warm steam rising from it.
This time, instead of the purple liquid from before, it contained a clear and brilliant golden liquid.
A sticky yet clear energy circled inside the cup like honey.

"It's Moonlight Honey Tea. It's a tea that melts the thorns stuck in your throat."

"......"

The woman took the cup with trembling hands.
As the warmth transferred to her cold fingers, her shoulders shook slightly.

Carefully, one sip.
As the sweet and smooth liquid slid down her esophagus, the woman's tightly knotted brow loosened just a little.
Like frozen ground melting in the spring sunshine.

"I won't ask for your name. Instead, show me your heart."

Eun-jo spread the Tarot cards in a fan shape on the old velvet cloth.
The backs of the cards sparkled like constellations in the night sky.

"Pick just one card. Thinking of where it hurts the most."

The woman hesitated, then picked a card as if led by fate.
The card was carefully flipped over.

Under gray rain clouds, a picture of three sharp swords piercing a red heart.
**<Three of Swords>.**

"Ah..."

It was an image so intuitive and painful that even Ji-hyuk let out a low groan.
Three knives piercing the heart precisely.
Betrayal, an indelible wound, and unspeakable sorrow.

"You must have been in a lot of pain."

Eun-jo's voice was low and calm, but it contained deep empathy.

"Wounds stabbed by the person you trusted most don't heal easily. Especially when that knife is in the form of sharp 'words'."

The woman's shoulders began to heave.
She took a tablet PC out of her bag and wrote something with trembling hands to show Eun-jo.

[The team leader... stole my idea. He packaged my proposal as his own achievement and made me look incompetent and lazy in front of the director. I was... so keeping it unfair that I couldn't say anything.]

Typical workplace bullying and gaslighting.
Ji-hyuk frowned.

While the victim lost her voice, the perpetrator would have been laughing.
It was a situation that made his blood boil as a detective.
To solve it legally, evidence was needed, and it was obvious it would be a long fight.

But Eun-jo was different.
She looked at the woman's wet eyes, not the letters on the tablet.

"You didn't stay silent."

Eun-jo held the woman's cold hand.

"You swallowed it. The screams, the anger, the injustice. Because you didn't want to harm others, because you were afraid of making a noise... you swallowed all those sharp thorns alone. Your kind heart was stabbing you."

Thick teardrops that she had been holding back fell from the woman's eyes.

"That's why your voice isn't coming out. Because the thorns you swallowed are pricking your throat. You were scared you'd bleed if you tried to spit them out."

Eun-jo drew one more card.
An image of a solemn queen sitting on a high throne, holding a sword vertically upright.
**<Queen of Swords>.**

"Look at this queen. Her sword is not a weapon to stab others, but a tool to protect herself. The power to cut through lies and speak the truth. Cold judgment and the decisiveness to cut ties."

Eun-jo placed the card in the woman's hand.

"Now you have to spit out those thorns. Don't let them stab you; aim them at the people who hurt you. Because your voice is too beautiful to swallow, and your truth is too clear to remain silent."

The woman lowered her head and vomited out silent sobs.
Ji-hyuk silently handed over the tissue box next to the table.

He realized.
This place was not a cold interrogation room to catch and imprison criminals, but an emergency room to wipe the blood of victims and stitch up their wounds.
Eun-jo was dealing with a deeper place than the law, the invisible court of the heart.

(End of Episode 3)
