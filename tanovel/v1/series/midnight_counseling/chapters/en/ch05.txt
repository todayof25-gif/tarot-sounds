"Looks like it's going to rain again."

Ji-hyuk muttered, looking up at the night sky.
It wasn't in the weather forecast, but seeing how his knee ached, it was certain.
His left knee, injured during the fire 10 years ago, was more accurate than the Meteorological Administration.

"There are many guests on rainy days. Because wet people look for warm places."

Eun-jo replied while wiping a teacup.
Just as she said, the counseling shop door opened past midnight.

*Creeeak—.*

This guest was a bit peculiar.
An elderly gentleman in his 70s with a bent back.
He was wearing a worn but well-ironed suit and holding a rain-soaked fedora to his chest.
And although there was nothing at his feet, he left the door open carefully as if he had brought something with him.

"Welcome."

Eun-jo bowed politely.

"Excuse me... did my cat perhaps come here?"

The old man, Old Man Kim's voice was dry like rustling fallen leaves.

"A cat?"

Ji-hyuk looked around.
Not a single mouse, let alone a cat, was in sight.

"His name is 'Nabi'. His fur is white like snow, and only his right eye is blue, an odd-eye. He hasn't been seen for a few days..."

Old Man Kim took a photo out of his bosom with trembling hands.
In the faded photo, a younger Old Man Kim and a white cat were smiling happily.
But the date on the photo was 'May 2013'.
It was a photo from a whopping 12 years ago.
Considering a cat's lifespan, it couldn't be alive.
*Is he senile?* Ji-hyuk looked at the old man with pitying eyes.

But Eun-jo was different.
She came out from the counter, bent her knees, and reached out toward the old man's empty feet.

"He's a pretty child. His fur is really fine."

As if there was a real cat there.

"Can you see him? Can you really see my Nabi?"

Old Man Kim's eyes went wide.

"Yes. He looks just like you, Grandfather. He's sitting very gently."

Eun-jo smiled.
Ji-hyuk got goosebumps.
Is this woman seeing things now, or is she playing along with the old man?

"Please sit. Nabi needs a place to rest too."

Eun-jo guided the old man to a table.
And she brought a cup of warm milk and catnip tea from the kitchen.
One for the old man, and the other for the empty space on the floor.

"Nabi... was not an ordinary cat."

Old Man Kim, having drunk the warm milk, slowly opened his mouth.

"10 years ago, when the big fire broke out in this neighborhood... everyone was busy running away. An old man like me just was ready to let the fire take me. I had no family, and no reason to live."

The fire 10 years ago.
Ji-hyuk's hand flinched.

"But Nabi... that little guy bit my pant leg and hung on. Telling me to get up. To live. He scratched my face and wailed. I lived thanks to that guy. I crawled out of the flames on all fours."

Tears well up in Old Man Kim's eyes.

"But... a few days ago, Nabi appeared in my dream. He kept walking backward. If I tried to follow, he ran away, and if I stopped, he waited... It seemed like he kept trying to take me somewhere."

Eun-jo nodded and took out Tarot cards.
Her touch was more cautious than ever.

"Shall we see where Nabi wants to take you, Grandfather?"

The card Eun-jo picked was **<The Moon>**.
Two dogs barking under the moonlight, and a crayfish crawling out of the water.
Uncertainty and anxiety, and the world of the unconscious.

"The moon illuminates the night, but sometimes it makes you lose your way."

Eun-jo's gaze headed toward somewhere in the empty air, not the card.

"Nabi doesn't want you to follow him, Grandfather. He wants you to stop... and let him go."

"Let him go...?"

"Walking backward means he wants to move forward but keeps looking back because he's worried about you. Because you are still trapped in that fire 10 years ago due to guilt."

Old Man Kim's shoulders trembled.
He had been suffering from the guilt of surviving alone, rather than the relief of surviving.
Nabi was the embodiment of that guilt.

"Nabi crossed the rainbow bridge a long time ago. On that night 10 years ago."

Eun-jo's cruel but gentle truth.
Old Man Kim looked blank as if shocked.
But soon he dropped his head and began to sob.

"I knew... I knew. I couldn't find the body... but there was no way he could have survived in those flames. But I couldn't admit it. Because it felt like I killed him..."

"It's not your fault. Nabi was happy that he saved you. The reason he stayed by your side until now was because he was afraid you would be lonely... he couldn't bear to leave."

Eun-jo pointed to the catnip tea placed on the floor.
Small ripples formed on the surface of the tea. As if an invisible tongue was licking the tea.

"Please say goodbye now. So Nabi can go peacefully too."

Old Man Kim reached out into the empty air.
He seemed to feel a warm temperature.

"I'm sorry... and thank you. Goodbye, my baby..."

At that moment, the air inside the counseling shop felt lighter.
Like a window that had been tightly shut had opened.

"He's gone."

Eun-jo gave a lonely smile.
Old Man Kim cried for a long time before getting up from his seat.
His bent back seemed to have straightened.

"Thank you. I think... I'll be able to sleep a bit now."

The seat the old man left.
Ji-hyuk blankly looked at the empty teacup on the floor.
He didn't see it.
But he could feel it.
The trace of someone's desperate heart having stayed.

And the question about Eun-jo's identity only deepened.
Does she really just do counseling?
Or is she... really a 'witch' who sees the invisible?

(End of Episode 5)
